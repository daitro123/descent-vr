# The level curve to 20

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by:

## Question

What does the whole road from level 1 to about 20 look like, so each zone knows its slice?

- Which levels each zone covers after Oakvale's 1 to 5, and how the cap rises.
- XP to each level, and how the kill and quest XP rules scale.
- What every level brings: a talent point from level 2 (decided), base abilities on some levels, attributes on all. How many base abilities per class by 10 and by 20.
- Today each level adds 20 health and 20% damage to you and to enemies of that level. By level 20 a linear step is almost five times level 1. Keep it, flatten it, or hand it to attributes.
- Whether Oakvale's numbers change once the curve is set.

## Answer

Settled on 2026-09-30 **by Claude on Tom's behalf**, taking the recommended option at every fork; the reasons are under Comments. The numbers are starting points, to tune on the headset.

- **Oakvale's levels 1 to 5 stay exactly as built.** Nothing in Oakvale's numbers changes.
- **Zones and caps:** Oakvale 1 to 5, the next zone 5 to 10, then two more zones of five levels each to 20. The cap rises to a zone's top level only once that zone has quests and camps; until then it stays 5. This map specs every class through level 10, ready for when the next zone's content comes.
- **XP:** the same step continues. Level L needs 100 × (L − 1) more XP, so levels 6 to 10 need 500 to 900 more (5,500 in all to 10) and level 20 needs 19,000 in all. A kill still pays 10 XP per enemy level (times its role), and a quest pays about half to a whole level of its zone. That keeps roughly ten equal-level kills per level all the way up, with quests the bigger share.
- **Grey enemies:** an enemy five or more levels below you pays no XP, so going back to Oakvale at level 10 doesn't farm. On Oakvale's own route this never happens (the farm is level 1 and you leave at 5).
- **Health and damage keep today's linear step,** now produced by attributes (see [Attributes and what they do](08-attributes-and-what-they-do.md)): without gear, a character has 100 + 20 × (L − 1) health and deals 1 + 0.2 × (L − 1) times level 1's damage, 280 health and ×2.8 at level 10, 480 and ×4.8 at 20. Enemies of each level take the same step, so an even fight feels the same at every level. Gear adds on top.
- **What every level brings:** attributes at every level; a talent point at every level from 2 (9 by level 10, 19 by 20); and base abilities at some levels.
- **Base abilities:** each class gets five by level 10, at levels 2, 3, 6, 8 and 10 (Oakvale's War Cry at 2 and Earthshaker at 3 are the warrior's first two), then two more at 14 and 18 in the sketch to 20. Talents add up to two more abilities by 20 (see [Talent tree rules](10-talent-tree-rules.md)). That's about nine abilities at 20, inside the four per hand plus one button per hand the research found players manage.
- **Levels 4, 5, 7 and 9 bring no ability,** only attributes and a talent point, so every level still brings a choice.

## Comments

**2026-09-30, on Tom's behalf:**

- Linear, not compounding: a compounding step (×1.2 a level) reaches ×32 by 20, so enemies a few levels apart would be hopeless or trivial. The linear step keeps an even fight the same and makes a level gap matter less as you climb, which the grey-enemy rule and camp levels cover.
- Five levels per zone: Oakvale set the pace at about a level per place, and five levels is roughly one zone's worth.
- Abilities at 2, 3, 6, 8 and 10: Oakvale's unlocks stay put, and the gaps widen as talents take over the choices.
- The cap waits for content: raising it with nothing to fight would only let XP pile up in Oakvale.

**2026-09-30, building ticket 17:** the rule above makes 4,500 XP in all to level 10, not 5,500 (100 + 200 + … + 900); 19,000 to 20 is right. The game uses the rule.
